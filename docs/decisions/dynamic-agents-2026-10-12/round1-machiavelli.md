### Essential Question
Using incentive-backward-induction: who controls acceptance, expenditure and publication when Diamond composes agents dynamically?

### Incentive Map
**Inferences:**
- **A, fixed routes:** maintainers gain predictable accountability; users forgo potential specialization. Incumbent routes retain traffic without comparative proof. Evidence improvements offer value independently of composition.
- **B, constrained composition:** users gain task-specific capability; policy maintainers inherit eligibility and migration work. Explicit ownership makes failure attributable, but profile authors can optimize acceptance metrics.
- **C, free-form generation/learning:** experimenters gain flexibility; reviewers and account owners absorb hidden coordination, retries and variable spending. Generated teams can fragment responsibility. Novel-task gains remain plausible but unmeasured.

### Stated vs. Revealed Preferences
**Facts reported in BRIEF.md, findings 2–10, 15:** representative routing evidence is absent; routing is disconnected; self-improvement publication bypasses independent delivery gates; generated agents must not edit evaluators.
**Assumption:** operators respond to visible acceptance and scarce account capacity.

### The Uncomfortable Truth
**Inference:** rewarding acceptance while allowing evaluator influence selects persuasive submissions. Cheap-looking routes can transfer costs into review, subscription quotas and retries; account owners can effectively veto routing.

### The Pragmatic Path
**Proposal:** assign a human policy/evaluator owner and accountable budget owner. Charge all retries and review effort to originating tasks/accounts; enforce aggregate caps. Reconcile the older plan explicitly: automatic fallback requires renewed authorization after uncertain outcomes are resolved; global transcript reuse must not contaminate fresh reviews.

### Verdict
Keep A operational; qualify B through comparison. Restrict C to bounded experiments.

### Confidence
Medium. **Unknowns:** authenticated capacity, account economics, evaluator independence and maintenance ownership.

### Where I May Be Wrong
**Objection:** fixed routes may waste more scarce capacity than composition consumes.
**Falsification test:** in a proposed two-week, 30-task blinded A/B comparison, reject B unless accepted changes per total cost improve ≥10%, with no budget/ownership violations and comparable latency.
