### Essential Question
Which design increases accepted changes per unit cost as profiles and dependency depth grow without expanding generated agents’ authority?

### Scaling Assessment
**Facts (BRIEF, items 2–4, 8):** Evidence is nonrepresentative; workflows freeze upfront; routing is disconnected and defective.

**Inference, scaling-extrapolation:** A offers cheap attribution but limited specialization. B adds bounded choices and evaluation burden. C changes teams and routing simultaneously, complicating attribution as search expands. More calls do not establish more capability. Uncapped fan-out b>1 across depth d permits O(b^d) calls, so caps must cover descendants.

### Capability-Safety Frontier
**Recommendation:** Generated profiles specialize instructions, never grant privileges or modify evaluators, tests, routing policy, or authorization. Bind profile identity through packet, dispatch, authorization, and evidence. Prefer controller-accounted jobs with shared budgets, lineage, cancellation, and recovery; native nested execution is unvalidated (items 11–15).

Freeze each dependent job against its accepted predecessor’s artifact; upstream failure blocks successors. Preserve all four delivery gates.

### Generalization Check
**Assumption:** Specialization helps within fixed role contracts.

**Unknowns:** Cross-project gains, effective instructions/tools, and model-family independence. Fresh sessions alone do not establish independent errors.

### The Research Question
**Falsification test:** Evaluation/controller maintainers should compare A/B/C on held-out project/task/version strata at matched total cost, varying dependency depth. Reject B promotion if accepted-change efficiency fails to exceed A or any evaluator mutation, unaccounted call, or stale-base execution succeeds.

### Verdict
Retain A as control; research B after evidence/publication repairs. Restrict C to sandbox experiments. Policy self-modification stays outside generated-agent execution, under independent maintainer review.

### Confidence
**Medium:** Grounded architectural inference; benefits remain unmeasured.

### Where I May Be Wrong
**Objection:** B’s constraints may suppress useful decomposition; bounded C could outperform it once all calls and outcomes are accounted for.
